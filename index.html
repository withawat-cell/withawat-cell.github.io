Terahertz Engineering Laboratory

Engineering the terahertz spectrum for sensing, imaging, and communications.

Terahertz spectrum

The terahertz band spans 0.1–10 THz, between microwave and infrared, and remains one of the least-exploited regions of the electromagnetic spectrum. Its vast underutilised bandwidth offers opportunities for ultrahigh-speed wireless communications, while distinctive molecular responses enable spectroscopy, atmospheric observation, and material identification. Its short wavelengths, combined with penetration through many non-metallic materials, enable high-resolution non-destructive evaluation. Our research focuses particularly on 220–330 GHz, which aligns with an atmospheric transmission window for extended-range transmission.

In-house engineering

Our research spans terahertz hardware, from lenses, metasurfaces and antennas to detectors, substrateless integrated platforms and complete systems. This in-house engineering capability complements our research in 6G communications, non-destructive evaluation, spectroscopy, and atmospheric sensing.

Lab history

Established in 2018 under the Australian Research Council (ARC) Linkage Infrastructure, Equipment and Facilities grant LE180100003, with co-contributions from RMIT University, University of Wollongong, and The University of Sydney. Since then, ARC Discovery Projects grants DP170101922, DP180103561, DP220100489, and DP240100162, along with other funding, have reinforced our work in standoff sensing, integrated platforms, and 6G communications.

Looking ahead

Our research spans strategic and applied work at the intersection of microwave engineering and optics, consolidating techniques from both domains to push terahertz technology towards practical, real-world applications.
